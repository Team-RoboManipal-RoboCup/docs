# Kinematics & Dynamics

## Overview & Objectives

The automated kinematics and dynamics calculation engine acts as a mathematically rigorous software pipeline that bridges standard robotic CAD models (URDF) with the absolute Product of Exponentials (PoE) framework. Utilizing absolute Forward Kinematics at the zero-pose, it maps spatial topologies to bypass fragile sequential Denavit-Hartenberg parameterizations.

The objective is to calculate the exact worst-case static and dynamic torques required across the manipulator's entire workspace to drive hardware actuator selection and validate mechanical viability without relying on exhaustive grid-search approximations.

## Technical Specifications & Bill of Materials (BOM)

- **Kinematic Framework:** Product of Exponentials (PoE) spatial vector algebra.
- **Dynamics Solver:** Recursive Newton-Euler formulation (two-sweep iteration).
- **Optimization Engine:** Stochastic global optimization via `scipy.optimize.differential_evolution`.
- **Topology Requirement:** Strict serial chain. Parallel linkages are programmatically filtered out during directed graph traversal to maintain solver compatibility.

## Design & Architecture

### Rigid Body Island Lumping

Standard URDF formats frequently split structural mass across intermediate fixed links (e.g., servo motors, mounts, or end-effectors). To preserve exact kinetic energy invariance without crashing the recursive Newton-Euler solver, the architecture executes a directed graph traversal. It mathematically shifts stationary masses into their parent active degrees of freedom using Lie algebra Adjoint transformations.

For a Center of Mass (CoM) frame offset from the parent link frame, the $6 \times 6$ spatial inertia matrix $G$ is shifted using the inverse transformation $T_{cl}$:

$$
G_l = [\text{Ad}_{T_{cl}}]^T G_c [\text{Ad}_{T_{cl}}]
$$

### Dynamic Payload Wrench Calculation

To simulate realistic payload handling, the space-frame gravity vector is dynamically rotated into the local end-effector frame using Forward Kinematics at every timestep of the optimization loop. The resulting spatial wrench:

$$
\mathcal{F}_{tip} = [m_x, m_y, m_z, f_x, f_y, f_z]^T
$$

encapsulates the exact cantilevered moment arm induced by the payload geometry.

### Global Torque Optimization

The multi-DOF torque landscape is highly non-convex, possessing local maxima generated by trigonometric gravity and Coriolis couplings. The pipeline deploys a Differential Evolution genetic algorithm to avoid computationally prohibitive exhaustive grid sampling. It treats the torque limits as $N$ independent optimization problems, searching the configuration space to maximize the required force at each specific joint:

$$
\text{Minimize: } -\vert \tau_{joint\_i} \vert
$$

### Validation & Crosscheck Pipeline

A dual-computation approach isolates topological URDF export bugs. An automated parser translates the XML structure into spatial matrices, while an independent manual script evaluates identically hardcoded spatial matrices. If identical kinematic states yield deviating torque arrays, the parser flags a mass dropout or misaligned spatial Adjoint shift.

## Software/Hardware Interface

### Core Dependencies

- `modern_robotics`
- `yourdfpy`
- `scipy.optimize`
- `numpy`

### Execution Sandbox

CPU multiprocessing (`workers=-1`) is strictly sandboxed inside an `if __name__ == '__main__':` block to prevent OS-level pickling crashes during the Differential Evolution evaluation pool.

### Closed-Loop Verification

Computed output torques ($\tau$) are fed backward into the Forward Dynamics solver. A successful parsing requires the resulting joint accelerations to match the target input accelerations exactly ($\ddot{q}$).

## Future Work / Known Limitations

### Time-Parametrized Trajectories

Current optimization assumes constant kinematic bounds. Future iterations will ingest continuous cubic or quintic polynomial trajectory splines to evaluate precise $\theta(t)$, $\dot{\theta}(t)$, and $\ddot{\theta}(t)$ path profiles.

### Sim-to-Real Friction Gaps

The recursive Newton-Euler solver assumes an idealized rigid body. Viscous and Coulomb friction coefficients, alongside physical motor constants ($K_t$), must be cascaded into the final outputs before feeding direct current commands to the motor drivers.
